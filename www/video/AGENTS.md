# www/video: the films

Every film is made from the real instrument or the real engine: walkthroughs
are recordings of the app, illustrated films are drawn on a deterministic stage
from the engine's own data, the music is scored for Auracle's voices and
played by its engine, and the narration is synthetic (Kokoro, offline) and
checked by speech recognition. Rules for the whole repo are in
[`../../AGENTS.md`](../../AGENTS.md); how the pipeline fits together is
[`docs/architecture/films.md`](../../docs/architecture/films.md); every tool is
listed in this directory's `README.md`. The `film` skill walks the steps.

## Layout

| Path | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| `films/<name>/` | One film: `script.json` (lines and beats), `storyboard.md`, `timeline.json` and `arrangement.json` (from the voice), `shots.json` (walkthroughs, usually generated by `gen_shots.py`), `film.js` + `cards.js` (the stage), `index.html` |
| `films/lexicon.json` | Shared pronunciations and ASR aliases. Edit it in one read-modify-write; several films share it. |
| `sound/` | The scores: `bloom` and `reach` (the marks) and `n3` (the bed), generated by `make sound` from `www/brand/sound.json`; and `signal` and `study`, the beds of the films not yet moved to N3. There are no cues (ADR-014) |
| `stage/` | The motion stage (`stage.js`), the illustration kit (`kit.js`) and the walkthrough player (`walk.js`) the films draw with |
| `voice/` | Kokoro TTS (`tts.py`) and the speech-recognition round trip (`asr_check.py`); its Python environment is set up per `README.md` § Setting up |
| `tools/` | Everything else: voice, record, rehearse, check, score, mix, render, publish |
| `out/` | Renders, gitignored. Only finished films are published, to `www/landing/assets/film/`. |

## Rules

- **The film tells the truth about the app.** A line is only true if the
  recording shows it. When a rehearsal finds the app short of what the guide
  or the narration says, report it and fix the **app**; change the words only
  when the app is right
  ([ADR-004](../../docs/decisions/004-descriptions-stay-true.md)). Numbers,
  patch names and percentages on screen come from the seeded session:
  re-check them after any engine change, or keep the narration from quoting
  them.
- **The spoken voice is [`www/brand/voice.md`](../brand/voice.md)'s**
  ([ADR-013](../../docs/decisions/013-one-voice.md)):
  - the narrator beside you, never a presenter;
  - writing for the ear;
  - explain, pause, demo, continue, with nothing played under speech;
  - the cold open before any words.

  It wins where `VIEWS.md` or `SCRIPTS.md` differ.
- **The films' sound is [ADR-014](../../docs/decisions/014-the-films-sound.md)'s:**
  - no cues;
  - one key (F) and one room;
  - Bloom in and Reach out;
  - the N3 bed;
  - the ladder and the grammar in `docs/notes/sound-2026-09/SPEC.md`.

  Plan-006 brings the tools to it. The values live in `www/brand/sound.json`,
  never in a generated score or `tools/sound_defaults.py`. `make sound`
  writes the cast (each part's preset, voices, trim, transpose and knobs, the
  lead's bend times, the drone's breath) into the scores, and the mix's
  values into `sound_defaults.py`. The rest of its numbers and pitches (the
  pedal, the lead's legato and swell, the bed's voicings, burble and sighs,
  the demo) describe the record scores' notes: `make sound` does not change
  the notes, and `make dev-check` fails while sound.json disagrees with them.
  It also fails on a number as a film tool's `--music-db`, `--duck-db` or
  `--gain-db` default, or as a `MUSIC_DB`/`DUCK_DB`/`APP_DB` fallback in a
  pipeline's shell code.
- **Rehearse before recording.** `tools/rehearse.sh <film>` dry-runs every
  shot and summarises lateness and errors. A film is ready when every shot
  passes with no errors, `tools/validate.mjs` is clean, `tools/framing.py`
  shows no callout leaving the frame and the voice passes its ASR gate.
- **Rehearsals and recordings run alone.** Every rehearsal and recording goes
  through `tools/one_browser.sh` (first come, first served, exclusive lane);
  `rehearse.sh` and `walkthrough.sh` already do. Browser tests share the
  machine two at a time, never beside one of these (ADR-010). Record on a
  quiet machine: no builds, suites or renders at the same time. Only recording needs it: `record_films.sh` logs
  "quiet window over" once every take is in, and the renders and encodes
  after that can share the machine.
- **Wait on the app, not the clock.** A shot waits for a state (`until`, a
  stamp) rather than sleeping, and a cut (`clips`) removes a wait the viewer
  should not sit through. A hold that ends at a stamp still presses at its
  `at` time; only the release waits.
- **A shot plays what the film cast** (RFC-007 part 2): a preset from the
  shortlist in `www/brand/sound.json`, through `shotgen.CAST` and
  `pick("cast")`, and an offer grown from one. `shotgen.dump` refuses
  anything else unless the shot's `"uncast"` says why (a line names the
  preset or describes its circuit, so it changes only with the script), and
  `tools/test_shotgen.py` holds every generated walkthrough to it.
- **Sessions are seeded, and a taught set-up re-deals the duel.** The first
  pair is dealt while the pool is still filling, the pair behind it is dealt
  ahead as soon as its sounds are in, and a pair is dealt again when one of its
  patches leaves the pool, so how many pairs have gone by when the pool is
  full depends on timing. The engine deals them in the same order every time,
  so a taught session (`shotgen.taught`) presses skip until the pair on the
  table is the fifth one dealt, each skip once the pair behind it has been
  dealt (`shotgen.REDEAL`, which reads the deals `shotgen.INIT` watches).
- **Disk is the limit.** A five-minute film renders about 4 GB of frame parts.
  The renderer keeps parts and lists them (`picture.ffconcat`) instead of
  joining them. Record films one at a time and delete a film's parts once its
  MP4 and WebM exist.
- **Voice changes re-time the film.** After editing lines, run `voice.sh`,
  then the film's tail/timing script, then `gen_shots.py`, then rehearse again.
- **A demo is laid out on its measured tail.** A line that hands over to the
  instrument carries `"demo": {"id", "play_s", "tail_s"}` (README § A demo).
  Measure each demo's tail with `tools/demo_tail.py` into
  `out/<film>/demos.json`. `voice.sh` and the films' timing scripts pass it
  to `timeline.py --demos`, and `publish.py` refuses a film laid out on an
  estimated tail. A film on N3 then gets its bed and marks from
  `tools/fit_score.py --film`, which `illustrated.sh` and `walkthrough.sh`
  run for it.
- **Publishing writes the site.** `tools/publish.py <films…>` copies the
  finished files into `www/landing/assets/film/`, fills the guide's
  `<!-- film:NAME -->` markers, the Films page, the README and the app's film
  chip. Chapter names (`CHAPTER_NAMES`) and silent loops (`VIEW_LOOPS`) for
  each film are set in `publish.py`.

## Commands

| Step | Command |
| --- | --- |
| Shared sounds, once | `make film-sounds` |
| Voice a script | `make film-voice FILM=<name>` |
| Rehearse a walkthrough | `make film-rehearse FILM=<name>` |
| Record and render a walkthrough | `make film-record FILM=<name> POSTER=<seconds>` (`SHOTS=a,b` re-records only those; `DRAFT=1` for a fast review encode) |
| Several: record all, then finish each, with previews | `make film-record-all FILMS="<name> <poster> <name> <poster>"` (`DRAFT=1`) |
| A 720p preview to send for review | `make film-preview FILM=<name>` |
| Render an illustrated film | `make film FILM=<name> POSTER=<seconds>` |
| Publish | `make film-publish FILMS="<a> <b>"` |
